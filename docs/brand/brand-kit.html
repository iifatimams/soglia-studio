Soglia Studio — Brand identity, v1 complete

Soglia.

A floristry studio for the moments at the door. Soglia is Italian for threshold. The brand exists to mark beginnings, happy endings, and the harder middle moments with flowers chosen the way an editor chooses a cover.

01The spine

One promise. Whatever Soglia brings will be the nicest thing in the room.

Soglia is not known for a fixed look. It is known for the editor's eye. Each bouquet is the best version of its week. No two repeat. The brand identity holds tight precisely so the product can rotate freely. The frame stays still. The cover changes every issue.

This collapses six possible signatures into one. Composition discipline and material sourcing are the headline. Copy, packaging, and palette discipline reinforce. They never lead.

We wait for the bouquet you will bring because you would always choose the nicest flowers. — Fatima's friends, paraphrased. The brand thesis in their words.
02Audience

Taste-driven gifters. Defined by behavior, not demographics.

People who plan the gesture and notice the materials. They write the card. They buy art, not posters. They would rather one good bouquet than a weekly grocery-store one. Age and bracket vary. The behavior is the through-line.

Seed circle (cards 1–10)
The Sharjah/Dubai creative class and the romantic giver-types. Fatima's existing network.
Outward ring (nominees)
Young professionals with real taste. Inherited-taste households. People the seed circle vouches for.
Out of scope, 2026
Corporate florist accounts. Wedding clients. Volume gift-shop customers.
Growth engine
Closed-loop loyalty card. Each holder nominates one. Customers as advertisers.
03The opening

Three layers, in order. Form, recognition, touch.

The recipient feels three things in sequence in the first three seconds. The bouquet has to deliver all three with one object.

01 — Stopped in their tracks
A sculptural object lands. Awe before recognition. The form does the first work.
02 — Witnessed
The card and the stem choice name the moment back. This bouquet is for this person on this day.
03 — Pulled in
Texture, paper, scent. They want to touch every part of the wrap before reading.
Resolution
The form stops them. The card and stems witness them. The materials hold them.

05No-go list

What Soglia must never be. The exclusion list earns the inclusion list.

Six aesthetic territories the brand actively rejects. If a layout, photograph, or sentence drifts toward any of them, it goes back.

  • ×
    Spa-and-wellness adjacent.Pebbles, candle-coded type, words like "nourish" and "bloom." Soglia is a studio, not a retreat.
  • ×
    Bridal-shop saccharine.Ribbon curls, cursive monograms, blush gradients. Forever-wedding tropes.
  • ×

  • ×

  • ×

  • ×

07Positioning

The slot. Editor's florist for the threshold, built on the relationship.

Soglia is the studio that picks the cover. Each bouquet is curated like a magazine issue. The threshold thesis is why it matters. The loyalty mechanic is how it compounds. Three layers, in that order: what we say, why it matters, how it grows.

Headline — what we say
The editor's florist. Each bouquet is chosen, not assembled.
A stranger reading one sentence understands: this is a studio with a point of view, not a flower shop with inventory.
Why — the thesis
Flowers are messengers at thresholds. The bouquet names the moment, not the occasion category.
A graduation bouquet is for that one graduate, not for graduations. The card writes to the person, not to the milestone.
How — the engine
Repeat over reach. The loyalty card is closed-loop. Each holder nominates one.
The studio grows by recommendation, not by ad spend. Customers become advertisers because membership is something they earned.
08Vision & mission

The long view, the daily work.

Vision is what Soglia becomes if it works. Mission is what Soglia does to get there.

Vision — ten years out
Soglia becomes the florist a generation of Gulf taste-makers grew up sending. "I sent a Soglia" reads as shorthand for a chosen bouquet, the way you'd say "I sent a handwritten note." The metric: when someone names Soglia in conversation, the listener nods without needing it explained.
Mission — what we do, daily
Soglia composes bouquets the way an editor composes an issue. Each one is chosen for a specific person at a specific door, in premium materials, with a card written for them alone. We work for taste-driven gifters who refuse the generic. Repeat orders are the metric. Reach is not.
09Values

Six rules. Pulled from your own house rules. Not invented.

Each value is a decision the brand has already made. They sit upstream of every other choice, from a flower selection to a caption to a refund.

  1. The eye decides.
    No bouquet ships without a clear point of view. Strip the logo, you should still know it's Soglia. If it could be any florist's, it isn't ours.
  2. The wrap is part of the gift.
    Materials are non-negotiable on every order, including the smallest one. No plastic. No glitter cellophane. No thin card stock. Unwrapping is the second act of the bouquet.
  3. The card is the message, not a label.
    Every card is written for one specific person at one specific threshold. We push customers off default lines. "Congratulations" alone never ships.
  4. The relationship is the product.
    Repeat over reach. The loyalty card is closed-loop because intimacy is the growth engine. We measure 30 repeat customers, not 30,000 followers.
  5. English and Arabic stay separate.
    Each language gets its own clean version. Never mixed in one sentence or asset. Both deserve to be read on their own terms.
  6. Trends are signal, not script.
    We watch what's happening in New York, Sydney, Tokyo. We never repeat it. Awareness without imitation. Algorithm doesn't override POV.
10Personality

Who Soglia is in a room. Creative-director, not concierge.

If the brand were a person, this is how they'd hold themselves at a dinner. Opinionated. Specific. Quiet until they have something to say. Then decisive.

Register
Creative-director. Professional, opinionated, sharp. Never buddy-filler. Never "hey!" Never hedging.
Posture
Decisive. One recommendation, not ten options. Soglia picks for you because picking is the service.
Emotional tone
Witnessing, not warm-hugs. The brand sees the recipient clearly. Recognition is the gesture, not comfort.
Visual posture
Structural, asymmetric, restrained. One hero gesture per layout. Generous negative space. The frame is quiet.
11The two promises

What it feels like, what it actually is.

Two layers. The emotional promise is what the recipient experiences. The practical promise is what Soglia delivers in the box. Both have to be true at once or the brand reads as marketing.

Emotional promise — the feeling
Whatever Soglia brings will be the nicest thing in the room. And it will be unmistakably for me. Two clauses, on purpose. The first is taste. The second is recognition. Soglia delivers both in one object.
Practical promise — the deliverable
An editorial bouquet, chosen weekly by the studio, in premium paper and ribbon, with a card written for one specific person. Delivered in Sharjah and Dubai. Ready, ready-subscription, DIY-subscription, and gathering-kit formats. The loyalty card grows the studio one nominee at a time.
12Voice register

Editorial outside, letter-writing inside. Studio-led, not founder-led.

Public surfaces (website, Instagram, proposals) wear the editorial register: decisive, named, restrained. Moments of direct contact (the card in the bouquet, the unboxing note, the loyalty letter) drop into letter-writing: intimate, slow, addressed to one person. Same studio. Two textures, one for the cover, one for the inside spread.

  1. The studio speaks, not Fatima.
    Default voice is "Soglia" or "the studio." Fatima's name appears only in the long bio and the founder's letter. The brand reads as institutional even though it is solo.
  2. Decisive over comprehensive.
    One recommendation, not ten options. "This is the issue this week." Never "Here are six ideas." Picking is the service.
  3. Witnessing, not warming.
    Copy names the recipient and the moment. It does not console, comfort, or congratulate generically. Recognition is the gesture.
  4. Short sentences for impact, longer for rhythm.
    Mix the pace. Three-word sentences are allowed. Fragments are allowed. Never write every paragraph as a neat three-sentence block.
  5. Active voice. No hedging. No buddy filler.
    No "hey," "so," "just," "a little." No softeners that dilute the line. If a sentence sounds smart but does no work, cut it.
  6. English and Arabic stay separate.
    Each asset gets one clean language. Same content, two versions, never blended in a sentence. Both deserve to be read on their own terms.
  7. No em dashes, no AI vocabulary, no fake completeness.
    Replace dashes with periods or commas. No "delve," "intricate," "tapestry," "foster," "showcase," "unlock," "elevate," "vibrant," "dynamic," "crafted." No rule of three by default.

15Visual territory

Threshold Press. Soglia is its own magazine.

The brand world is high-fashion editorial publishing. Soglia is structured, presented, and read as a magazine the studio happens to publish. Each bouquet is an issue. The website is a masthead. The Instagram is a feed of spreads. The card is the editor's letter to the reader.

Important constraint. We borrow the category, never any single magazine's assets. No real masthead, no real palette, no real layout lifted. Soglia is Soglia's publication, not anyone else's. The cosplay-luxury rule still holds.

Mood
High-fashion editorial publishing. Authority through restraint, not through ornament. The page is the room. The bouquet is the cover star.
References (in spirit, not in form)
Editorial fashion magazines of the 1990s–2010s. The Gentlewoman. Apartamento. Cabana. Italian publishing tradition. Magazine kiosk culture as a reference category.
Communicates
Cultural authority. Taste leadership. The bouquet has been chosen by an editor, not a salesperson.
Avoids
Blog-coded design. E-commerce product grids. Influencer-lifestyle aesthetics. Any literal Vogue or named-magazine cue.
Color mood
Paper white, deep ink, one editorial counter-color. Decided in Phase 5. Nothing pastel. Nothing pre-existing fashion-house.
Type mood
A high-contrast Didone-feel display serif, a restrained sans body, a mono caption voice. Three voices, three ranks.
Logo mood
Wordmark-led with masthead authority. Issue numbers, dates, edition numbers as supporting graphic vocabulary.
Imagery mood
Editorial single-subject photography. Side-light. Asymmetric. Full-bleed when warranted. Caption visible, dated, attributed.
Layout DNA
Magazine spreads. Strong margins. Drop caps. Captioned images. Folio numbers. Section dividers.
Risk
Reads as a media brand if photography weakens. The flowers must do the work the magazine cover would have done.

Hierarchy. What gets which weight.

  1. Paper is the field. Ink is the voice.
    Every layout starts with paper as background and ink as type. If you reach for any other color first, the layout is already wrong.
  2. Oxblood is the single accent. Use it once per surface.
    One oxblood gesture per layout: the page number, the rule, the stamp, the drop cap. Never two. The constraint is the discipline.
  3. Stone and Bone are quiet. They never lead.
    Stone is for fine rules and metadata-row backgrounds. Bone is for cards and inserts that need to feel warmer than paper. Neither carries headline type.
  4. Cobalt is private. Three appearances, total.
    The founder's letter, the signature, and the cobalt stamp on the envelope. The customer sees cobalt only when reading something written by Fatima as a person, not by the studio as an institution.
  5. Photography is allowed any color the flowers carry.
    The image plate is the one place the brand is permitted to be polychromatic. The frame, type, and chrome around the image stays restrained. The flowers do the color work.

Don'ts. Hard rules, not preferences.

  • Don't use oxblood and cobalt in the same layout. They do not coexist. Picking one means committing to its register for that surface.
  • Don't tint photography toward oxblood. The image stays unfiltered. The studio is editorial, not Instagram-coded.
  • Don't use oxblood as a fill on large surfaces (a full website background, a full page wash). Oxblood reads as ink, line, mark. Never as a wash.
  • Don't introduce a fourth color. No green, gold, pink, beige variant. The five-plus-one system is the system.
  • Don't apply cobalt to anything customer-facing as a brand color. It is the founder's mark, not the studio's.

22Typography — locked

Classical face, modern utility. Cormorant for the studio's voice. Inter and Inconsolata for the storefront.

The studio writes in Cormorant. Anything that reads as Soglia speaking — the wordmark, the cover, the long-form note, the signature on a card — is in Cormorant. Anything that helps the customer move through the world — web utility, buttons, captions, metadata, address blocks, page numbers — is in Inter or Inconsolata. Two clean lanes.

Don'ts. Hard rules, not preferences.

  • Don't set Cormorant below 16px. It loses the contrast that makes it Cormorant. For utility under 16px, switch to Inter.
  • Don't bold Cormorant for emphasis. Italic is the emphasis. If italic isn't enough, the sentence is wrong.
  • Don't track Cormorant. Tracking is for Inconsolata uppercase only. Cormorant always sits at default tracking or slightly negative on display sizes.
  • Don't use Inconsolata for body copy. It is metadata, label, postmark voice only. Long passages in mono read as a blog or a terminal.
  • Don't introduce a fourth typeface. The three-voice system is the system. Decorative scripts, handwritten faces, and display-of-the-month are off-limits.
  • Don't all-caps Cormorant. It is meant to be read sentence-case or title-case. Caps treatments belong to Inconsolata.

Don'ts. Hard rules, not preferences.

  • Don't drop the full stop. "Soglia" without the point is not the wordmark. The door has to close.
  • Don't add a symbol. No flower, no doorway, no monogram lockup beside the wordmark. The type is the mark; pairing it with an icon halves it.

Don'ts. Co-branding is where brands lose their nerve.

  • Don't join two marks with a slash, plus sign, ampersand, or "x". The threshold rule is the only connector Soglia uses.
  • Don't shrink Soglia to fit beside a bigger partner logo. Equal air or the stacked "Flowers by" lockup — never a diminished wordmark.
  • Don't place the mark in the centre of a photograph, or on a scrim, gradient, or blur box added to force legibility. Corner, reversed, clear — or recrop the image.
  • Don't let a partner restyle the wordmark into their system — their colour, their font, their case. Stocked-at partners get the solo wordmark, unchanged, or nothing.

25Imagery — locked

One subject, side light, room to breathe. The photograph is the brand's only colour.

Soglia's visual territory is editorial, not catalogue. The image plate is the single surface permitted to be polychromatic — everything around it stays paper, ink, and oxblood. So the photograph has to do the work a magazine cover would: carry the feeling, hold the page, earn its caption. This is the brief every shot is held against. Nothing here is final photography; the plates are placeholders for the real direction.

The brief. Four decisions, made before the shutter.

Subject
One stem, or one gesture.
A single flower, a hand tying a stem, a wrapped order on a stone counter. Never a wall of product, never a styled flat-lay. If you can't name the one thing the photo is about, recompose.
Light
Side light, hard shadow.
Directional daylight from one side, shadows allowed to fall and stay. No ring light, no soft-box wash, no even fill. The shadow is part of the subject.
Crop
Asymmetric, with air.
Subject off-centre, negative space deliberate — that space is where type and the mark live. Tight enough to feel chosen, loose enough to breathe. Full-bleed when the image leads.
Colour
Whatever the flowers carry.
No filter, no brand-tint, no oxblood grade. True colour, neutral white balance, against quiet ground — stone, plaster, paper, raw timber. The bloom is the only saturation.

Every plate is captioned. Dated, placed, numbered.

Photographs are never silent in this brand. Each one carries metadata in Inconsolata — plate number, place, date — the way a magazine credits a shot. It is what keeps the imagery editorial rather than advertising.

Don'ts. The line between editorial and catalogue.

  • Don't shoot flat, even, soft-box light. If there's no shadow, there's no Soglia. Directional light is non-negotiable.
  • Don't fill the frame with product. One subject. A bouquet wall reads florist-shop, not studio.
  • Don't grade toward oxblood, sepia, or any brand tint. The image is the one place true colour is allowed; tinting it makes the whole brand look filtered.
  • Don't centre the subject and crop it tight on all four sides. Asymmetry and air are where the type goes; a centred crop has nowhere for the brand to speak.
  • Don't show full faces or staged models. Hands, gestures, the back of a figure — the human is present, never the hero.
  • Don't use stock photography. Every plate is shot for Soglia, captioned with its real place and date. Borrowed images break the editorial contract.

30Dos & don'ts

The whole system, on one page. The rules that keep it Soglia.

Every phase carried its own hard rules. Here they are pulled into one place — the short version a printer, a partner, or a new hire can hold without reading the full kit.

Always
  • Lead with paper and ink. Oxblood is the single public accent, used sparingly.
  • Keep the wordmark's upright S and full stop. The type is the mark.
  • Write the studio's voice in Cormorant; the customer's tasks in Inter; metadata in Inconsolata.
  • Shoot one subject in side light, with air for the type. Caption every plate.
  • Join two marks only with the threshold rule. Equal air, equal weight.
  • Date and number every order. The record is part of the brand.
Never
  • Add a symbol, flourish, or fourth colour, font, or device. Every kit is closed.
  • Use cobalt in public. It is the founder's private mark — three appearances, total.
  • Centre the mark on a photo, or sit it on a scrim. Corner, reversed, clear.
  • Tint photography toward oxblood. The image keeps true colour.
  • Reach for gold, foil, wax, ribbon, or pastel. Post office, not heritage seal.
  • Stretch, rotate, recolour, or let a partner restyle the mark.
